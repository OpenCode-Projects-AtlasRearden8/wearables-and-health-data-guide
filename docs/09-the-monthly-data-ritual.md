# The Monthly Data Ritual

Here's the secret this book has been building toward: **the wearable's job is to collect quietly; your job is to look gently and occasionally.** Checking graphs daily produces noise and anxiety. Checking them monthly produces knowledge. This chapter gives you a hospitality-standard version of that monthly look — about thirty minutes, once per month, with a warm drink and no sense of urgency.

Reasons to believe in the ritual:

- It **matches the questions that actually matter.** Trends live at the scale of weeks and months, not Wednesday afternoons.
- It **protects you from the 2 a.m. graph-loop** described in the [honesty chapter](03-what-the-numbers-honestly-mean.md). If checking heats anxiety, you learn to check at a *scheduled*, supported moment instead.
- It makes the [care-team page](05-sharing-trends-with-your-care-team.md) and the [privacy checklist](11-privacy-checklist.md) happen *automatically*, because they're steps 3 and 5 on the same calendar.
- It's **ADHD-friendly by design**: a fixed format, a printable worksheet, a time limit, and a defined "done." Low-energy months can shrink it to five minutes without guilt (the checklist below marks which steps are skippable).

## When and where

- **When:** the first or last Sunday of the month, or any recurring slot your life already holds steady. Calendar it with a recurring 30-minute block named something kind — "data tea."
- **Where:** wherever a printed [data-decode worksheet](10-data-decode-worksheet.md) lives comfortably — kitchen table, desk, clinic break room. Screen for the app graphs, paper for the notes.
- **Bring:** the printed worksheet, something to drink, and (once a quarter) the privacy/passcode mood you use for the [privacy checklist](11-privacy-checklist.md).

## The ritual, step by step

### 0. Before you sit: the fresh-eyes question (30 seconds)

Say out loud (or write at the top of the worksheet) the one question you care about this month. "Am I sleeping more consistently?" "Is my resting heart rate stable?" "Am I moving less since the desk change?" **One question only.** The ritual exists to serve it; everything else is cargo to skim.

> If no question comes, that's fine — default question: "Compared with last month, what's *different*?" The worksheet has a slot for the answer.

### 1. Collect: open three screens (5 minutes)

- **Resting heart rate, 30–90 days** — the single most informative line most wearables draw on a consistent wearer.
- **Sleep timing (lights-out/wake), the month view** — not stage colors yet; the *window shape* first.
- **Activity, the month view** (steps or active minutes).

That's it — three screens. Everything else is optional this month. If you use the `vitals-logbook-mcp-server` companion for home measurements (cuff, thermometer), its `trends` tool prints you the same shape of summary for those readings in seconds; drop that next to the watch app's graphs and the workshop table will be set.

### 2. Read: what changed, what didn't (10 minutes)

Fill in the worksheet's middle section:

- **Stable things** — the baseline behaviors you can rely on. List them: "resting HR has stayed in my usual band," "lights-out hovers near 11."
- **Changed things** — anything meaningfully different, with dates if you can find them: "third week of the month, sleep got fragmented; matches the project deadline + two late coffees."
- **Unexplained change lasting 2+ weeks** — this is the one bucket you *do* act on, by [writing it on a page for your care team](05-sharing-trends-with-your-care-team.md). Not by acting on it yourself. A change that hangs around is a question for a professional, and the ritual is what's shaped that question into words.

> **Resist the re-investigation urge.** The ritual is not a net to trawl for new problems. If the worksheet slots fill up with anomalies, that's a *finding* about the month, not a reason to scroll further tonight — the data will still be there next month.

### 3. Adjust: one knob, maximum (5 minutes)

Pick **at most one** practical adjustment patterned off the month's finding:

- A notification or goal change: [setup chapter](02-setting-up-your-watch-kindly.md) rules.
- A battery-anchor fix: [battery chapter](08-battery-habits.md) table.
- A family-sharing tweak: [family chapter](06-family-sharing-and-kids-accounts.md) guardrail.
- Nothing at all — "keep everything as-is" is a legitimate conclusion that most months deserve.

One knob. Two at most on a chaotic month. More than that and you can't tell which change did anything anyway.

### 4. File: make history yours (5 minutes)

- Put the filled worksheet in a folder — paper or scanned, either works; a shoebox of them is a year's story.
- **Once a quarter** (mark it in the calendar), run the umbrella [export](04-exporting-your-data-safely.md) and the [privacy checklist](11-privacy-checklist.md) instead of steps 3–4's usual glide.
- Note anything you'd want your own chart to remember but which the gadget can't (good days, sick weeks, travel): one line in the notes box.

### 5. Close: say the closing sentence

End every ritual out loud or in writing:

> **"I looked, I noticed, I made one change (or zero), and I don't need to look again until next month."**

This sentence is the entire mental-health warranty of the wearable era. The device is now a quiet instrument, the month's history is on paper, and you're free until the next date.

## The low-energy version (5 minutes, no guilt)

For weeks when thirty minutes is fantasy: do steps 0 and one item of step 1 — the **resting heart rate page only if you need a look at it**; otherwise the ritual *can* be just the fresh-eyes question plus the closing sentence. A five-minute ritual done twelve times a year is the same habit as a thirty-minute one, and no one needs to know the difference but you.

## Variations for particular wearers

- **Shift workers:** run the ritual against *your* rolling weeks, not calendar months, and read sleep data as naps-plus-nights (a nap is not a failure; the [honesty chapter](03-what-the-numbers-honestly-mean.md) treats them as real windows).
- **Families:** the ritual can be a shared fifteen minutes — adults with their own worksheets (no peeking at each other's [non-consented pages](06-family-sharing-and-kids-accounts.md)), kids with a sticker page if they want one. Shared maintenance encourages everything, shared dashboards were never the point.
- **Caregivers:** your version includes step 3 for the person you help (one knob) and a quick eyes-on their wearing consistency; see the [family chapter's eldercare section](06-family-sharing-and-kids-accounts.md). Keep *your* worksheet and *their* worksheet separate, even if one feeds the other.

## Twelve months later: what this habit gives

Follow the ritual a full year and you'll own something genuinely uncommon: **a year of single-page self-knowledge**, written in your own hand, anchored in real dates — the kind of record that makes appointments better (chapter [five](05-sharing-trends-with-your-care-team.md)), device switches painless (chapter [four](04-exporting-your-data-safely.md)), and anxiety small. A shoebox of worksheets outlives any gadget you'll ever wear.
