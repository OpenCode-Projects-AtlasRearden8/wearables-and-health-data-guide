# Welcome: What This Manual Is (and Isn't)

You put a little computer on your wrist. Now it vibrates, glows, and tells you things: your heart rate, your sleep score, how "recovered" you are, how many steps beats yesterday. Some of it is fascinating. Some of it is confusing. Some of it, if you're honest, is a little anxiety-making.

This manual is the calm friend who explains the whole thing in plain English.

> **The one promise this manual makes to you:** everything here is *education*, not advice. We explain what the numbers mean, how the devices work, and how to handle your data safely. We never tell you what a number means for *your* body, and we never suggest you change a medication, a workout, or a treatment because of a spreadsheet. That conversation belongs to you and your own care team. When a number worries you, the right move is always to bring that number up with a real clinician who knows you — never a manual, an app, or an AI.

## Who this is for

- **You just got (or about to get) a smartwatch or smart ring** and want a kind, unhurried setup instead of a 40-message notification avalanche.
- **You already wear one** and want to know which of the numbers are trustworthy, which are vibes, and which ones are worth writing down.
- **You help someone else with their device** — a kid, a parent, a partner — and you want family sharing, kids' accounts, and privacy settings explained without a single condescending metaphor.
- **You care about your data** and want to export it, back it up, and share sensible summaries with your own care team on purpose — not by accident.

If you're a nurse or other healthcare worker, you'll notice this manual respects your training: it never plays doctor, it just keeps the machines explained and the paperwork tidy.

## How this fits with the vitals logbook

This manual deliberately does **not** walk you through logging blood pressure, weight, or temperature readings at home. That job is handled by its companion project, `vitals-logbook-mcp-server` — a small local tool from the same **Asdale Dynamics, LLC** family that keeps a tidy logbook file on your own computer and produces simple CSV exports for your care team.

The split is simple:

- **The wearable measures it automatically?** (heart rate, sleep, steps, temperature trends) — this manual explains what those numbers mean and how to handle them.
- **You measure it yourself with a cuff or thermometer?** (readings you take deliberately) — the `vitals-logbook-mcp-server` companion keeps the logbook, trends, and exports.

In the chapter on [sharing trends with your care team](05-sharing-trends-with-your-care-team.md), we'll show you how the two fit on one page without either tool doing the other's job.

## How to read this book

Three honest suggestions:

1. **Skim the table of chapters, then read in whatever order your life allows.** Everything is written to stand on its own.
2. **Do the monthly ritual chapter even if you read nothing else.** It's the habit that ties the whole book together.
3. **Print the worksheet and the checklist.** Chapters [10](10-data-decode-worksheet.md) and [11](11-privacy-checklist.md) are designed to work on paper, with a cup of coffee, away from the screen.

One last thing before we start. Notation you'll see through the book:

- **Verify first** — every vendor feature name differs between brands and app versions, so where this book says "look for a setting like X," treat the label as a treasure hint, not an exact address.
- **No numbers will be named** where a range would send you spiraling. A manual can tell you what a mess means; only your care team can tell you what a measurement means for you.
