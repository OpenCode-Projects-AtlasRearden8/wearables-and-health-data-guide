# Setting Up Your Watch, Kindly

A brand-new wearable arrives as a bundle of good intentions: it wants to greet your mornings, win your ring, close your circle, and high-five your sleep. That enthusiasm is charming on day one and exhausting by day three. Setup — done kindly — is mostly about deciding, *in advance*, who gets to interrupt you and when.

This chapter walks a first-day setup in beginner-friendly order. The exact menu names differ between Apple, Garmin, Fitbit, Samsung, Google, Oura, Whoop, and everyone else — so treat each step as "find the setting where this lives" rather than "click this exact button." The [glossary](12-glossary.md) decodes any unfamiliar words.

## Step 1: Un-box like you're defusing tension

Nothing here is urgent. Put the kettle on.

1. **Charge fully before doing anything clever.** Most devices ask for this anyway, and it means the setup won't die halfway through. See [battery habits](08-battery-habits.md) later for the long-term rhythm.
2. **Pair from the phone app, not the phone's settings menu** (and typically not from a tablet). Every wearable has a maker app — that app is the brain; the watch is just the wrist. Download it first, create the account second, pair last.
3. **Say yes to the health-permission screens *once, thoughtfully*.** During pairing you'll be asked to share health data between the watch app and the phone's health store (Apple Health, Health Connect on Android, or the maker's own store). More on this decision in the [privacy chapter](07-privacy-settings-worth-touching.md) — but the short version: sharing between *your own* devices is normal and useful; sharing with *other people or other companies* is a decision you make deliberately, and can always take back.

> **Beginner note:** "Health store" is the plumbing, not an app you need to babysit. Apple Health on iPhone and Health Connect on Android are just file drawers on your own phone that your other health apps file data into, and read from. You control the permission slips.

## Step 2: Fit and wear — the *actual* accuracy settings

Here's a secret appliance-industry fact: **wearing position beats every other setting** for measurement quality. The optical sensor in a watch or ring shines green/infrared light into your skin and reads what bounces back. Loose fit equals shaky readings; so does wearing it over a tattoo or low on the forearm like a bracelet.

- **Watch:** snug enough that it doesn't slide "a finger's width" up and down the arm during a handshake, loose enough that your skin isn't dented at bedtime. About one finger of space between strap and wrist is the classic advice.
- **Orientation:** most watches prefer the face on the inside-ish underside of the wrist — follow the app's guided picture during pairing, and no two brands agree exactly. Re-check this after a week of wearing; the app will often quietly guess wrong on day one.
- **Which wrist?** The one you'll actually wear it on. Comfort and consistency beat every theoretical accuracy gain. A watch worn daily on the "wrong" wrist produces far better data than a watch that lives in a drawer.
- **Tell the app which wrist you chose**, and update it if you switch. Both the step counting and the optical readings are calibrated for it.
- **Rings:** scale your finger at room temperature (fingers swell), pick the size that's snug without throwing off your typing, and follow the maker's guidance about which finger matters.

If your device supports it, enable **wrist detection / lock on removal** later — it also stops nudges while the watch is on the charger.

## Step 3: Measure a real baseline, not a fantasy goal

Activity goals arrive from the factory tuned for a marketing photo, not for your actual week. The kindest setup move is to **let the device observe you for two ordinary weeks before you accept or change any goal.**

- Turn *off* auto-advance or "goal coaching" for now, if offered.
- Do your week. Errands, naps, the job, the toddler, the desk.
- After two weeks, run the [monthly data ritual](09-the-monthly-data-ritual.md) once, *early*, and set your goal near what an ordinary week actually looks like, with roughly a ten percent stretch — not the goal an influencer set.

Goals are calibration tools, not verdicts. A goal you miss every day is a motivational demolition device. A goal you meet most days teaches your brain that streaks are survivable. There is no correct number of steps that applies to a whole species.

## Step 4: Retire the notification avalanche

By default, most pairings mirror *every* phone notification to the wrist. Undo that:

- In the watch app's notification settings, **start from zero**: turn off mirroring entirely or deselect everything, then re-add only the categories that deserve wrist time — messages from people (not apps), calendar, maybe one or two specific apps.
- Set the quiet-hours equivalent first (do-not-disturb, sleep mode, theater mode). Position them so they'll cover your sleep window and your focus hours, then add exceptions for the two people whose 2 a.m. call you would *want*.
- Turn **off** "health reminder" buzzes that aren't helping: stand reminders, hydration reminders, and hourly-move nudges are a matter of taste; you can always re-enable a single one later once you know your own pattern.
- Enable **silent (haptic) alerts** for alarms and timers you actually use — a vibration watch makes an excellent, non-glaring alarm and kitchen timer.

> **A nurse-friendly aside:** shift workers deserve a paragraph. Sleep mode and move-recommendation features assume a 9-to-5 shape of day. Use the manual toggles rather than scheduled ones if your schedule rotates, and use the sleep chapter's honesty notes when interpreting a night sleep score after a post-nights nap.

## Step 5: Set the fitness bits to truthful

- **Height, weight, age, and sex field(s):** fill in honestly and completely — these feed calorie and pace estimates. If you're not comfortable storing something, prefer leaving it blank over inventing it; an estimate fed wrong numbers is worse than no estimate.
- **Left/right and wrist/hand selection** — see step 2.
- **Heart-rate zones:** accept the automatic zones for now. They're estimates keyed to age-based formulas; you can train yourself into correcting them later with your care team's input if you're an athlete. Don't hand-tune them on day one from a feeling.
- **Workout detection and auto-pause:** leave auto-detect on briefly to learn your actual habits, then refine. False "workout started" buzzes during dishwashing are a normal rite of passage.

## Step 6: Decide the data-sharing posture *once*, on purpose

This is the step most people skip, then regret at year three. In the watch app, look for anything named **Sharing, Connected apps, Permissions, Family, or Third-party apps.** The [privacy chapter](07-privacy-settings-worth-touching.md) and the [privacy checklist](11-privacy-checklist.md) walk through this in detail. The short version for setup day:

- Approve the sharing between your watch and your phone health store (Apple Health / Health Connect).
- Decline everything else until something you actually want knocks; grants are easy to give and easy to revoke.
- If a "share your activity with friends" feature sounds fun — great, it can be! Just turn it on deliberately for a *named* person, not "anyone who finds me."

## Step 7: Learn the three buttons, not thirty

Every wearable has about four gestures that carry ninety percent of daily life: **wake/see-the-face**, **start a workout**, **start a stopwatch or timer**, **open the payment/wallet step** (if you use tap-to-pay). Learn those; let the rest find you by accident. The user manual exists in the maker's app under something like "Help" — it's fine to read later, on a rainy Sunday.

## A first-week checklist

Copy these into your phone notes, tick them off gently:

- [ ] Charged fully, paired via the maker's app, wearing position set honestly
- [ ] Health-store permission granted for my own devices; other sharing declined for now
- [ ] Notification mirroring blanked out, then re-added: messages, calendar, one alarm
- [ ] Sleep mode and do-not-disturb scheduled around my real sleep window
- [ ] Goals left to auto-default; calendar note to revisit in two weeks
- [ ] Backup noted — phone app saves to the maker's cloud (see the [export chapter](04-exporting-your-data-safely.md) to learn what that means and how to escape it later)

If a step of the checklist doesn't apply — no payment chip, no sleep tracking wanted — crossing it out *is* the correct version of done.

## When setup goes sideways

- **Won't pair:** reboot *both* the phone and the watch, and factory resets are rarely needed; unpair-and-repair is the standard trick. Put the watch physically near the phone during pairing (phones prefer that more than marketing implies).
- **Setup crashes on older phones:** check the app's minimum OS version in the app store description *before* fighting it. Many companion apps quietly require recent generations.
- **Battery dies unreasonably the first week:** normal; the device is doing an inventory of your life (calibration workouts, sensor warmups, database sync). It usually settles by week two.
- **The app wants an account you can't log into:** if a maker-server hiccup is butting in, wait a few hours — account outages typically resolve without any action. Don't create a second account "to make it work"; two accounts is the most common way wearable users lose their data history.

If after a week the device is *still* making you anxious rather than informed — notifications off, goals kind, data sharing deliberate — revisit step 4. The whole point of setup is a machine that helps you live your week, not a machine that grades it.
